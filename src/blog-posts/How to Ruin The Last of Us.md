## Character Development and Writing Issues

### Ellie's Characterization Problems
Ellie just doesn't feel like Ellie to me. She doesn't seem filled up with rage and acts quite different than how game Ellie did. I don't think Craig Mazin really understands Ellie's character at all. The dialogue he writes between Ellie and Dina sounds so off for the situation they're in. 

There was a post on Reddit that said Bella works great as a supporting character, but not as a lead. And I can totally get behind that. I don't think she can really deliver as a lead, especially with the writing treating her like she's stupid and still somewhat childish. It feels like that version of her character from season 1 has been ported into this story instead of her character evolving in her mannerisms and thinking.

It didn't seem like Ellie was bloodthirsty for revenge at all, or at least not as much as I felt she should have. Her interactions with Jordan in the game help introduce that desire she has for revenge, but with it absent in the show, you don't get any of that. At this point, I felt Dina actually cared more about getting revenge for Joel. Her monologue was amazing. Isabella Merced kills it, but I think she kills it too much. It feels like almost every moment, Dina takes charge. It feels like this was Dina's show and not Ellie's.

### The Competence Issue
I really don't like how much Ellie is getting bit or being called stupid. Like she's not incompetent. There are so many times where the characters are calling Ellie stupid, when she's not supposed to be. I also don't really like how much Ellie has been getting bit. In the games, if you get bit, it triggers a death sequence, because it doesn't matter whether Ellie is immune or not, she can still get ripped apart and get mauled to death. I just really don't like how careless they're making her. In my head, she's a survivor. She's been training. She should know how to protect herself and not get bit in most circumstances.

I felt the no guns rule was a very simple thing to understand and didn't like how much time they spent on that and how it affected the representation of Ellie's character. When they're sneaking up to the building where they eventually find the stalkers, Dina is in the lead and calling the shots. And when they're inside the building, Dina has to remind Ellie not to fire a gun unless absolutely necessary.

### Acting Performance Assessment
I think Bella Ramsey's acting is fine, but I feel more of the blame should go on Craig Mazin, the main writer of the show. If there was a better script in terms of how Ellie is portrayed, there'd be a lot less criticism on that. The flashback episode just showed me how engaging Pedro Pascal is as a lead, and then switching back to Bella, she just doesn't give the same effect. I don't think she can deliver fully on being a lead.

## Story Structure and Narrative Decisions

### Season Length and Pacing Issues
Seven episodes was a pretty poor decision for an even longer game. It just feels so rushed. I absolutely knew that seven episodes was a horrible decision and have no idea why they went this route. They actually could've asked for as many episodes as they wanted; Neil Druckmann and Craig Mazin said they could tell the story they wanted to tell with seven, which is such a bummer because there was a big opportunity to make something much better and less rushed.

From one standpoint, it really only matters about allocation. Ellie Day 1-3 is 3-4 times longer than Jackson. The flashbacks are around the same as Jackson. No matter how many episodes, I'd expect this ratio of section lengths to be preserved, but giving Jackson the same amount of time as Ellie Day 1-3 tips the scale of things. It doesn't feel like you spend much more time with Ellie in Seattle than you did with her in Jackson, when it most definitely should.

### The Three Month Time Jump
The three month time jump was pretty dumb. It loses all sense of urgency, and I don't really understand how/why Ellie wouldn't go immediately. The show is entitled to make creative liberties, but the three month time jump I don't feel added anything and instead harmed the narrative. Since they time jumped three months ahead, you lose that sense of urgency.

## Significant Story Changes

### The Pregnancy Revelation Scene
The most glaring change was how Ellie reacted after Dina told Ellie she was pregnant. I just found it jarring how Ellie made out and had sex with Dina immediately after the reveal, while game Ellie essentially does the opposite thinking Dina is a burden. Not saying it's bad to change things, but I just don't see why Ellie would do that when she's thrust into the exact same situation as game Ellie. It made me kind of annoyed at the direction they're taking Ellie and how different she is to her game counterpart.

The show can take its own liberties for sure, but judging by the episodes, it seems like they're telling the same story, so I just felt the change very out of place. This is where adaptations like this one thrust the characters in pretty much the exact same scenarios and the exact same situations, so if something is different, it's just hard for me to wrap my head around why they wouldn't just do what they did in the game.

### Joel and Ellie's Relationship
Ellie says that she didn't make up with Joel and says that she never talked with him on the porch. Unless they're not actually going to do that scene (which would be a terrible decision), then I feel audiences that haven't played the game just might be confused when that scene eventually comes. In the game, Ellie says that she and Joel are good; she doesn't outright deny that anything happened. I don't know what they're gonna do there.

### Tommy's Character Arc
Tommy not going after Abby first was an extremely poor decision. The fact that Tommy couldn't do anything to save his brother really hits, but if he's going to show up later in Seattle with Jesse, I don't see any reason to not have him go first. It's virtually the same. Having him go to Seattle first adds another sense of urgency too - finding and catching up with Tommy.

### The Mel and Owen Sequence
I didn't care for how they changed the Mel sequence. When Ellie kills Mel/Owen in the game, she purposely kills both of them, and feels all the more guilty killing a pregnant woman. In the show, she only intends to kill Owen and she accidentally kills Mel. I personally feel this shifts some of the blame off of her, whereas the game undoubtedly makes it clear that this is Ellie's mess she's caused. There's behind-the-scenes footage of what looks like them filming a version of the scene the way it goes down in the game, so I don't know why they changed it.

## Missing Content and Missed Opportunities

### Cut Locations and Sequences
I really miss Hillcrest and Capitol Hill and all the other stuff they've been cutting out. There were so many moments I wanted to see come to life. Like the Eastbrook Elementary and Capitol Hill areas and the dialogue about Dina and her family going to synagogues when they're in the Dome. Her run into Jesse too and the sequence that follows - that sequence where Jesse drives that car while Ellie shoots WLFs and infected until they drive into the lake would've been jaw-dropping to see on screen.

Hillcrest especially, that part is so sick; seeing Ellie kill WLFs would've further cemented her into this arc of destruction that she's set herself on. The Hospital sequence was insanely rushed in the show. I loved seeing Ellie swim through their base, kill the PS Vita girl and sneak up on Nora in the game. All that, once again would've showed how far she's willing to go, and at this point in the show I wasn't buying it.

### Character Development Opportunities
Her interactions with Jordan in the game help introduce that desire she has for revenge, but with it absent, you don't get any of that. Not showing Ellie infiltrate into the hospital doesn't help show what she's willing to do for revenge but it also just introduces a lot of questions. The Hospital is guarded; how did Ellie get in unnoticed? The fact we never saw anything there was pretty jarring to me.

I wish they would've adapted Ellie climbing through the vents hearing Nora listening to "It was a Nice Day" - absolutely loved that introduction and it would've made it seem even more like Nora was one of the "good ones" until she whipped out "that little bitch got what he deserved."

### Unnecessary Additions
Ellie's Seraphite encounter was totally unnecessary. I know they originally planned it for the game, and then scrapped it, but I don't know why they decided to include it here. They could've cut it out and nothing would change. Also, if/when that happens to Abby next season, I feel people that haven't played the games are just going to think that they're just pulling the same thing again.

Apparently that sequence was originally created for the game but they ended up taking it out because it didn't fit. I have no idea why they added it back in here since it clearly doesn't work. Her wanting to save the Seraphite was just kind of out of the blue, especially after being attacked by them earlier. In the game, Ellie doesn't give a shit about the Seraphites at all and just thinks they're just people in the way like the WLFs.

## Production Elements

### Visual and Technical Aspects
The set design for the show is pretty insane. It's so good and looks pretty much exactly like the game sets too. They were thought out so well. I think I've got more of an issue with the costume department. All of everyone's outfits look too clean and pristine and don't feel worn out.

### The Standout Episode
The flashback episode was by far the best episode this season and was really good! Neil Druckmann directed/wrote that one and it definitely shows. It was led more by Pedro Pascal than Bella and he killed it. I thought the porch scene was great and Pedro and Bella's acting shined so well here! Pedro's facial expressions were great. And hearing that quiver in Bella's voice when she's talking about having a purpose was fire.

## Information Reveals and Narrative Execution

### Premature Story Revelations
I DIDN'T like how Nora just exposition dumped all that Abby's father info on Ellie and everything. I also didn't like that about the earlier episodes with Abby saying all of that to Joel. I just felt it wasn't needed, and was hoping that we'd learn more details about it when we see Abby's stuff. That's one of the biggest reveals in the game and I felt they just wasted it with Abby's dialogue and Nora's dialogue.

Ellie saying "I know" I felt also ruined a big reveal that hit so much harder for me in the game, watching her become more disconnected and skeptical of what Joel said to her. And we don't get that confirmation that she knows until that flashback, which is such an emotional and unforgettable scene. But in the show, that confirmation is in the Nora scene. I don't think it was a terrible decision, but I think when you have such a great blueprint to follow (the game), everything that gets changed, if it's not because of budget cuts or production restraints, then I feel it better be good.

### Structural Issues
Nora accidentally falling down into the spore infected area just doesn't have the same impact of Ellie choosing to bring her down with her. I know in the show the spores just got introduced and no one knows about them, but still. I liked the parallel between Ellie looking at the pipe the same way Abby looked at the golf clubs, but the execution felt off.

## Character Performances and Supporting Cast

### Jesse's Character
I liked the conversations between Ellie and Jesse, even though Jesse in the game has the complete opposite stance and tells Ellie that he would've gone with her too if she asked. I didn't see a problem with it while watching, so I can't be that bothered by it. I do like how he was portrayed. He felt like the most realistic and grounded character out of the bunch to me. And I think his character differences definitely work here because of the Ellie that they've created here too.

### Other Performances
I thought Kaitlyn Dever did a great job as Abby. She even sounded the same I thought. I liked how they gave Isaac some backstory in the show, and how they adapted those notes about him in the game. That's some good stuff.

## Final Assessment and Overall Thoughts

This season wasn't it for me. I'm hoping they hear our criticism for this season and Bella Ramsey improves her skills for a better third season. Even though I feel this season lacks in a lot of areas, I still think this show is one of, if not the best video game adaptations yet.

The series was fine until that moment with Dina's pregnancy revelation. And then every episode (until the flashback episode) I noticed more and more problems. Season 1 got the characters right and even expanded/adapted some of them in different ways than the game that felt faithful. Season 1 also didn't have as much content as the game, but it was similar enough in pacing and story that it didn't matter at all or affect its ability to showcase that version of the story.

But season 2 just fails at portraying some of the most critical characters, and it's just hard for me not to be annoyed when they've got a perfect blueprint to go by. Given that this is an adaptation, and the first half of the game is already slightly longer than the first game, then I'd expect there to be the same amount of episodes as the first season or more. Choosing to adapt a longer story than the first season in fewer episodes with improper allocation is just a baffling decision to me.

Even some of the actors recognize the flaws. I definitely think this show is one of the most memorable episodes so far, even though I feel this season lacks in a lot of areas.
